# Catalog candidates from Gate C5 (PROPOSALS ONLY; `ARTIFACT_CATALOG.md` is not edited)

Six candidates, in the catalog's entry format. Numbers continue after #25 (#26-#31 are placeholders for the reviewer). Families are those of `ARTIFACT_CATALOG.md` "Families and synthesis": Aggregation-level mismatch, Validation that could not fail, Instrument cost and instrument blindness, Operational hazards. **Four fit Operational hazards; two (a, f) are a record-keeping hazard, which fits that family only loosely; a new family "Record integrity" (a log or report asserting something the primary record does not support) would hold #a, #f and arguably #23 (stale platform table).** Reviewer's call.

### #26 · A status log asserting a module state the journal contradicts *(candidate a)*
- **What happened (E6, found in C2-APPLY phase 2a):** `e6/E6_STATUS.md` at 19:36:53 on 2026-10-05 recorded "loaded stock 6284DA42 (refcnt 0)" and "Phase 4: isolated to multi-user.target (exit 0)". The journal shows the *verified specasync* module was loaded at 17:48:16, no unload before 19:36:53, and **no `systemctl isolate` command anywhere between 17:48:07 and 20:06:04** (the machine had been in `multi-user.target` since 17:48:07, when an isolate killed the session running E6). The log line's "(exit 0)" matches no journal record.
- **False conclusion it would have produced:** that the stock module was the resident module at the start of the Family 1 table collection, and that the isolate step ran as logged. Either would make the status log a primary record of platform state. Neither is supported.
- **How caught:** by a read-only re-account of the gap against the persisted systemd journal (`e7/E6_GAP_ACCOUNT.md`), not by the E6 analysis. No effect on E6's data: every E6 run reloads and verifies srcversion and parameters, so the resident module in the gap cannot have affected a run.
- **Evidence:** `results/analysis/gate_e/e7/E6_GAP_ACCOUNT.md` §1, §2, §4 ("Discrepancy"); `e6/E6_STATUS.md`. Unresolved from the journal: whether the second isolate ran as a non-journaled no-op or not at all.
- **Family:** new "Record integrity" (proposed); otherwise Operational hazards. Caught only by a later, independent pass.

### #27 · A helper with a hard-coded multiplicity correction reused across gates *(candidate b)*
- **What happened (E0.9b helper, E6, E7, E8):** `tests/e09b_analyze.py` computes `mde_bonf_s = Z_MDE_BONF * sd` with `Z_MDE_BONF` fixed for **alpha = 0.05/16**, E0.9b's family size (`e09b_analyze.py:18`, used at `:56`). Later gates import the helper unchanged. E4 (18 comparisons, labelled `MDE18`), E5 (`MDE3`), E6 and E1 print or write the column under their own family sizes' names while the number is still the 16-test one; E7 and E8 found it and report `mde_family_s` instead (`e7_analyze.py:13`; E8 pre-registration).
- **False conclusion it would have produced:** a minimum detectable effect stated "at the family alpha" that is not at that family's alpha: too optimistic for families larger than 16 (E4: 18; E8 and E7 have their own sizes, not re-derived here), too conservative for smaller ones (E5: 3). A reader would conclude a null result was better or worse powered than it was.
- **How caught:** by the author of E7 reading the helper while writing the pre-registration (documented in `E7_PREREGISTRATION.md` and the E7 status, "OPEN FOR REVIEW (DOC-class) (1)"). E6's report does not quote the column, so no reported number is wrong; E4's `MDE18` label and E5's `MDE3` label on this column (in `e4_analyze.py:108`, `e5_analyze.py:140`) have **not been audited** here and are the open question.
- **Evidence:** `tests/e09b_analyze.py:18,56`, `tests/e7_analyze.py:13`, `e7/E7_STATUS.md:21`, `E7_PREREGISTRATION.md:32-33`, `E8_PREREGISTRATION.md:43`; `e6/*.csv` `mde_bonf_s` column.
- **Family:** Aggregation-level mismatch (a constant tied to one family's size applied to another's); caught by a later pass.

### #28 · A build script that swallows a failure when a Git-LFS patch is only a pointer *(candidate c)*
- **What happened (E3a-2 build on the T4, 2026-10-09):** `driver/patches/*.patch` are stored in Git LFS (`.gitattributes: *.patch filter=lfs`). Without git-lfs they are 130-byte pointer files. `driver/scripts/reconstruct_build_tree.sh:34` runs `patch -p0 --forward -N < specasync_selective_apply.patch || true`; `patch` printed "Only garbage was found in the patch input", `|| true` discarded the exit status, and the tree was assembled **without the glue changes**. The first verified build failed in the compiler (`uvm_va_space_t has no member named specasync_pred`, 5 errors).
- **False conclusion it would have produced:** a misdiagnosed driver-source problem; had the build somehow succeeded without the glue (e.g. a module built from the pristine tree plus the specasync files), a module with a different srcversion would have been loaded and mistaken for the verified one. The srcversion check is what stands between the two.
- **How caught:** by the failing compile, then by reading the script output. The same hazard occurred earlier on the 5070 Ti ("3 `.patch` files show LFS-pointer-vs-real-content diffs" in `GPU_FREE_REPORT_2.md`; T4_REPORT: "git-lfs was not installed; installed it and confirmed all three patch files resolve to real content") and was handled by hand each time.
- **Evidence:** `results/analysis/t4/E3A2_T4_BUILD_REVIEW.md` "Setup problems" #1; `driver/scripts/reconstruct_build_tree.sh:34`; `.gitattributes`; `MIGRATION_NOTES.md`; `T4_REPORT.md:22`. C5 step 7a adds a pointer check to the script.
- **Family:** Operational hazards (a loud failure *eventually*, caught by the failure itself, but at the wrong place); also "Validation that could not fail" in the narrow sense that `|| true` made the step unable to fail.

### #29 · A session that ended itself by killing the login session containing it, and an orchestrator that died with its session *(candidate d)*
- **What happened (E7-T4, 2026-10-08/09):** (i) the previous session ran `loginctl terminate-session` on the remote-desktop (xrdp/XFCE) session that contained it, which ended that session (E7-T4 Amendment 2). (ii) Before that, the instance was powered off during the smoke pass, which "killed the orchestrator", and the pre-registered cap had to be re-set (Amendment 1). The same class occurred on the 5070 Ti in E6: `systemctl isolate multi-user.target` stopped `user@1000.service`, which SIGKILLed the tmux server that held the E6 session (`E6_GAP_ACCOUNT.md` §1, 17:48:23); the mitigation since is `loginctl enable-linger` plus a detached `systemd-run --user` unit.
- **False conclusion it would have produced:** none in a data result (no timed run was in flight in either T4 case); the hazard is lost time, a changed cap, and a session that ends mid-protocol, and the risk that a half-run gate is read as complete or as a "stop condition" outcome.
- **How caught:** by the failure itself (the session ended); recorded as an amendment before any timed run.
- **Evidence:** `results/analysis/t4/E7T4_AMENDMENT_1.md`, `E7T4_AMENDMENT_2.md` ("Second reason: the previous session"); `e7/E6_GAP_ACCOUNT.md`; `e7/E7_STATUS.md` ("enable-linger").
- **Family:** Operational hazards (peer of #11, #22-#24). Cause of the 2026-10-08 poweroff was **not established**.

### #30 · A pre-registration that checked VRAM but not host RAM *(candidate e)*
- **What happened (E7-T4, smoke row 25):** the pre-registered oversubscribed Stencil (`bench_stencil_oversub 48000 1`, 17.17 GiB managed) was sized by the **VRAM** ratio (1.144 of the T4's memory vs 1.078 on the 5070 Ti). The g4dn.xlarge has 15.4 GiB of RAM and no swap; UVM CPU-side page population exhausted host RAM and the kernel OOM-killed the process (`block_populate_pages_cpu`, file-rss 15.1 GB) at 2026-10-09 08:05:52 UTC.
- **False conclusion it would have produced:** none reached a result (no timed data existed). Had the cell been run with a larger timeout or a swap file, an OOM-thrashing run would have been timed as an "oversubscription" measurement.
- **How caught:** by the smoke pass (the pre-registered smoke exists for this), as a kernel OOM kill; then Amendment 2 removed the cell and added a stop condition (managed bytes < MemAvailable - 4 GiB). The E8 pre-registration (24 GiB array) **did** check host RAM ("minimum MemAvailable 30.4 GiB (> 12 GiB)") after this.
- **Evidence:** `results/analysis/t4/E7T4_AMENDMENT_2.md` (Reason); `e7t4_smoke.csv`; `e7t4_runner.py` `MANAGED_BYTES`; `E8_PREREGISTRATION.md` (host-memory sentence). The earlier near-OOM from a host leak is #11.
- **Family:** Operational hazards (peer of #11, "caught by the failure itself"); more precisely a missing pre-registration precondition.

### #31 · Status-log times typed by hand as estimates *(candidate f)*
- **What happened (E7, E8):** status-log lines carried a time that was typed from memory instead of printed by `date` or a script. In `e7/E7_STATUS.md`, "14:20 Phase 3" and "14:05 Phase 2 DONE" run later than the real times (Phase 2 finished about 14:00; the Phase 3 push was at 14:04:37 by `date`). E8's lines had the same problem and were corrected to the real clock; E7's were left as is.
- **False conclusion it would have produced:** that the session cap and the E6/E7/E8 skip rules (which are computed from wall-clock deadlines) were applied against the times in the log; and a reader of the log would reconstruct a longer or differently ordered session than the commits show. The orchestrators compute deadlines inside the script from `phase0_start.txt`, so the **decisions** were not affected, only the narrative.
- **How caught:** at the E8 review, comparing the log with `date` outputs and git commit times (the commit times are authoritative).
- **Evidence:** `results/analysis/gate_e/e8/E8_STATUS.md:8`; `e7/E7_STATUS.md` ("14:20 Phase 3", "14:05"). The rule since: times come from `date` or the orchestrator. C5 followed it (this session's log times were printed by `date`).
- **Family:** new "Record integrity" (proposed); otherwise Operational hazards. Caught only by a later pass.
