## Design

- **n = 10 per cell**, 12 cells (6 arm/L combinations × 2 workloads), **120 runs**.
- **Run order:** `e09b/sweep_order.csv`, generated by `tests/e09b_make_order.py`
  with seed **20260925**. It has 10 blocks, and each block is a seeded
  permutation of all 12 cells, so all arms, L values and workloads are
  interleaved. SHA-256 of the file:
  `45241ae6da59185e192bfa43e72037d5bfafda31d8e426148878d3b477b4c0d8`.
  The order is followed exactly.
- **Per run** (`tests/e09b_runner.py`):
  1. `rmmod` after checking that refcnt is 0.
  2. `insmod` with the arm's parameters.
  3. Verify srcversion and parameters.
  4. Diff dmesg.
  5. `specasync_clear`.
  6. Run `timeout T setarch -R <bench>`.
  7. Wait 1 s, outside the timed region.
  8. Snapshot all counters, MemAvailable, free disk and the dmesg diff, and check every stop condition.
  9. Append one CSV row to `e09b/sweep_runs.csv`. Commit every 10 runs.
- **Timeouts:** Stencil 22 s and GraphBFS 168 s. Each is 5× the largest
  pilot wall-clock for that workload, taken with arms pooled (4.357 s and
  33.521 s). Using the pooled maximum means the timeout is at least 5× the
  expected median for every arm, including C0 and the L values that were
  not piloted.
- **Resume:** if the session is interrupted by anything that is not a stop
  condition, the runner resumes at the next index not already in the CSV.
  The interruption is recorded in STATUS, and the sweep is never restarted.
  No cell is ever re-run.
- No decomp-ring dumps in the sweep (the pilot needed them; the sweep does not).

## Outcome of record

**Process wall-clock** is the `time.monotonic_ns()` difference around
`timeout T setarch -R <bench>`. It covers the whole process: CUDA init, and for
GraphBFS the CPU-side graph build, which dominates its roughly 33 s. The
benchmark's self-reported `[RESULT] Time` is recorded but is not the outcome
of record.

## Primary family — 16 comparisons (baselines stated)

For each workload (2) and each L (4):

1. **C6-L vs C0.** Can perfect first-touch knowledge, at any lookahead, beat
   the driver as it ships?
2. **C6-L vs C1.** What is the marginal effect of speculation with
   prefetching off?

## Statistics

- The median is the statistic of record. Delta = median(C6-L) − median(baseline),
  in seconds and in %.
- **Two-sided Mann–Whitney U**, with `scipy.stats.mannwhitneyu`. It uses
  `method="exact"` when the 20 values have no ties, and `"asymptotic"` (with
  continuity correction) otherwise.
- **Holm–Bonferroni step-down across all 16 comparisons**, family α = 0.05.
- **Cohen's d** uses the pooled standard deviation.
- **MDE at achieved n** = (z₀.₉₇₅ + z₀.₈₀) × √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂). This is the
  normal approximation, with two-sided α = 0.05 and 80% power. The MDE at the
  Bonferroni level α/16 is reported alongside it.
- **Outliers:** Tukey fences (1.5 × IQR) are computed within each cell. Every
  comparison is reported both with all runs (primary) and with flagged runs
  excluded from both cells. The without-outliers analysis gets its own Holm
  correction across the 16. **No run is ever removed from the primary analysis.**
- **Verdicts:** a Holm-significant comparison is labelled *C6 faster* or
  *C6 slower* by the sign of delta. Any other comparison is labelled
  *no significant difference*.

## Falsification trigger

**Any C6-L beats C0 by more than its MDE, Holm-significant, on either
workload.** Formally: baseline = C0, Holm-significant, and median(C0) −
median(C6-L) > MDE. If this fires, it is reported prominently at the top of
the report, and **nothing further is run**: no extra L values and no
follow-up cells.

## Prediction test (from the addendum, `46761ec`)

The measured saving is median(C1) − median(C6-L4096) wall-clock. It is
compared against the pilot servicing-window interval: Stencil-24K
[0.170, 0.217] s, point estimate 0.198 s; GraphBFS-23 [−0.0165, +0.0165] s,
point estimate ≈ 0.

The result is classified as **inside**, **below** or **above** the interval.
An *above* result means the ceiling was wrong, and the report will say so.

## Mechanism metrics (diagnostic only; never substitutes for wall-clock)

Each is the per-cell median over 10 runs, by workload and L:

- **Pre-staged coverage** = `spec_migrations` ÷ distinct pages (1,125,000 /
  287,956). This is an **upper bound**: Step 1 showed that `spec_migrations`
  also counts no-op `make_resident` calls on pages that were already resident.
- **Pipeline:**
  - `ft_predictions` → `enqueued` → `spec_migrations` (migrated).
  - **Not-enqueued**: `drops` (queue full at 1,024, or kzalloc failure).
  - **Lost-after-enqueue**: `enqueued − spec_migrations`.
  - The identity `ft_predictions = enqueued + drops` is checked on every C6
    run and reported.
- **`fault_already_resident`** (demand faults that found the page already
  resident on the GPU).
- **`spec_hits`**: **a 10 ms staleness-window counter, not a win counter.**
- `demand_faults`, `ft_skipped`, `ft_unknown_page`, `ft_exhausted` for context.

**Excluded:**

- **Prevented faults:** structurally zero under this driver, because the
  speculative worker never maps (E0.9a-2; Step 1 Q2).
- **Same-run usefulness:** 100% by construction under policy 6's skip rule,
  so it carries no information.

## Stated limitations

- **GraphBFS-23 runs a weakened oracle.** Policy 6's cursor jumps to one past
  the highest rank seen. A single out-of-order fault on a late-ranked page
  therefore skips every page between, which is why GraphBFS pre-stages only
  0.9–22.9% of pages (E0.9a-2; the pilot measured about 23–24% at L=4096).
  GraphBFS results understate what a first-touch oracle could achieve there.
- **`fault_already_resident` semantics:** it means the worker beat the
  *servicing thread*, not that the page was ready before the GPU accessed it.
- **Pre-staged coverage is an upper bound:** `spec_migrations` includes no-op
  `make_resident` successes (Step 1 Q1).
- **Structural asymmetry:** speculation can make a fault cheaper but never
  prevent it, while the stock prefetcher maps in the same service step and can
  prevent faults (Step 1 Q2). The C6-vs-C0 comparison is therefore not
  like-for-like in mechanism. It is exactly the question "can this design
  beat what ships".
- **Stencil queue saturation:** the pilot showed about 18% of C6-L4096
  predictions dropped at enqueue (queue full). This is part of the design
  under test and is reported, not corrected.
- **Platform:** all results are RTX 5070 Ti, driver 595.91.07, kernel
  7.0.0-34. The manuscript's platform table is stale (ledger entry, Step 0).

## Stopping rules (verbatim from the gate brief)

> ## Automatic stop conditions — check after every run
>
> If any of these occurs, **stop the whole session immediately**: no retries, no
> reload, no restoration of the desktop. Record it in `STATUS.md` with the exact
> evidence, commit locally, and end.
>
> 1. Any new `nvidia_uvm` line in `dmesg` containing `BUG`, `Oops`, `WARNING`,
>    `general protection`, `NULL pointer`, or `exited with irqs disabled`.
>    Diff `dmesg` against its state before each run.
> 2. `rmmod` fails, or `/sys/module/nvidia_uvm/refcnt` is not 0 before a reload.
> 3. A benchmark hits its `timeout`. A hang can mean a dead fault-servicing
>    thread — do **not** kill and continue.
> 4. `MemAvailable` below 6 GiB.
> 5. Free disk space on the results filesystem below 10 GiB.
> 6. The loaded srcversion differs from the one recorded in Step 0.
> 7. Anything that would require departing from the committed pre-registration.

Implementation note: the runner applies condition 1's pattern to **every** new
dmesg line, not only lines that name `nvidia_uvm`, because the first line of
an oops does not name the module. This is stricter than the rule and never
looser. A nonzero benchmark exit code, a failed `insmod` or a failed
`specasync_clear` is also treated as a stop under condition 7, since
continuing would depart from the design.
